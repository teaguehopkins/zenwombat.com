How Fixed Point was made

Play with your friends without being online at the same time.

Play

The idea

Each player records one track of a level. Everyone after them plays beside that recording and has to keep it alive. Ghosts can’t hear you or change course, but they can leave you cover, supplies and open doors. The cooperation is real. It just travels forward in time.

The hard problem

Each new wave is built against the recordings already locked in, aiming at the moments a ghost is carrying, hacking or alone. It has to be dangerous and still beatable, so the generator checks that one player can reach every threat in time. And the past has to replay exactly, so a timeline only locks if everyone in it survived.

What changed

People liked the concept more than the first version of it. Four-minute levels in four waiting slots made players hold back, and the phone controls didn’t explain themselves. Now there are 45-second rounds, sticks you can see, and a ghost beside you from your very first round. The original four-player runs are still there. It’s a public beta, and still changing.
