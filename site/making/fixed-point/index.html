How Fixed Point was made

How do you game with friends when you can’t game at the same time anymore?

Play

Problem

You used to be online together every night. Then came jobs, kids and time zones, and now finding one free hour in common takes a week of messages. The friendship is still there. The overlap isn’t.

Playing by turns is an old answer, but it usually means waiting for someone to move. We wanted the feeling of having fought beside a friend, when the two of you were never there at once.

Hypothesis

If each of us records one run of the same level, yours can play beside mine as a ghost. If the next wave is hunting that ghost, I have to learn how you play to keep you alive: where you pause, which door you take, when you’re exposed. Koster calls fun the learning of patterns. Here, the pattern to learn is your friend.

We thought that could feel like cooperation, even though it runs in one direction. You leave me cover without knowing I’ll need it.

Experiment

The first public version had four players, four slots and levels of up to four minutes. We shared it with friends and asked what happened. Marty Cagan separates value risk (will anyone want this?) from usability risk (can they work it?), and the answers split along that line. The idea got an enthusiastic yes. The version we’d built didn’t.

One person couldn’t work out the phone controls and gave up. Another left the open slot for someone else, and said the clears weren’t tense enough to share. A third liked it and meant to come back later. That was only a few people, but they agreed on the problem and stumbled on our solution.

Solution

We kept the problem and changed the answer. Rounds are now 45 seconds. Play drops you straight in beside a ghost, with the sticks drawn where your thumbs go. There are more layers to take, so nobody has to guard a seat. Each wave is hidden the first time and stays the same on every retry. A surprise becomes a pattern you can master.

It’s a public beta because the experiment isn’t over. We still have to find out whether short rounds are tense enough to share, and whether a ghost is enough company to bring you back. The answer we’re after hasn’t changed: play with your friends without being online at the same time.
