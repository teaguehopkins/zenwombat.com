How Legends of Loot was made

A whole game about the part before the game: every menu has a red dot, and clearing one makes more.

Play

The idea

A red dot on a menu means something new is buried inside, and you have to dig for it. Legends of Loot keeps that itch and throws away the game it was meant to sell. You clear notifications until there are none, in an arc that swells to chaos and then drains to zero.

The hard problem

Clearing a notification can set off others, so the run could easily loop forever or strand the player one dot short. Every notification in the main run fires at most once, and a snoozed one comes back at most twice. A bot then clears the game in random order: over 500 runs it finished in 159 to 179 clears, and every one of the 191 notifications could be reached.

What changed

Badge counts are never stored. Each menu counts the live notifications beneath it, so a parent can’t disagree with its children. Once the joke worked, a second pass gave it sound, music, card art and a proper moment at Inbox Zero, because the payoff has to feel earned even when the prize is nothing.
