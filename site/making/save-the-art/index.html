How Save the Art was made

What if, in a twin-stick shooter, you had to be precise, because the room around you mattered?

Play

Problem

Twin-stick shooters let you spray bullets in every direction, and most of them cost nothing. A miss vanishes into the scenery. The scenery is there to be wrecked, and wrecking it is half the pleasure.

We started by turning that around: what if you had to be careful about damaging everything around you? The rest grew from there. The room needed to hold something nobody would want to hit, so it became paintings. Paintings worth fighting over led to a stolen collection, in 1944.

Hypothesis

Koster describes fun as the mind mastering a pattern. Most twin-stick shooters ask you to learn one: where the enemy will be. If every bullet keeps going, there’s a second pattern behind the first, which is what the bullet hits next. We thought reading both at once could be more interesting than reading one faster.

It also let the rule carry the meaning. Nobody has to tell you that art is fragile. You learn it when you win a fight and look at the wall.

Experiment

The first test was one plain room, with simple shapes and bullets that kept going. It was too hard, a little too fast, and the art didn’t hurt enough to hit. We had aimed for chaos, and chaos drowned out the choice we wanted players to make.

Later tests showed us where the game could be solved without learning it. A riot shield was so strong it was always the right answer. Rushing in with a shotgun met little resistance. Koster warns that players will find the easy pattern if you leave one, and that a solved pattern is a dull one. We set a careful bot against a reckless one to see which way of playing the game was really rewarding.

Solution

We made the game slower and the stakes sharper. Fights became deliberate. Guards fall quickly but hit hard, and every bullet halves what’s left of a piece. Steady aim is the default. The shield now takes your sidearm’s place, and you can’t fire from behind it. On the standard difficulty, the careful bot now beats the reckless one from the first raid.

We also made most of the art minor work by unknown painters, so a real masterpiece stands out. When you save one, you learn its true wartime history. The shooting is pulp, and the record of what was stolen is sincere.
