How Save the Art was made

A shooter where the thing you’re protecting is behind the thing you’re shooting at.

Play

The idea

Every shot needs a backstop. Bullets that miss keep flying, and some go through whoever they hit, so you have to know what’s hanging behind your target before you fire. The strongest guns are also the hardest on the art.

The hard problem

Surviving art is crated for you, so the fight itself has to create all the risk. Guards hide behind easels, hold canvases up as shields, set fires and plant charges. Make too much noise, or let a guard reach the alarm, and the Burn Order starts a clock. The room keeps asking for a slower shot than it gives you time to take.

What changed

The first brief was chaos. The first playtest said it was too hard and a little too fast, and that art should hurt more to hit. So guards got weaker, everyone slowed down, and every bullet now halves a piece’s condition. It became a game about finding a clean line. Most of the art became work by unknown painters, too, so that a real masterpiece feels like the reason you came.
